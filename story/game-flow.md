# Game flow reference for the story designer (owner/main session, 2026-10-27)

Read-only for you. This is how the player acts today. Source: owner direction and tasks T55 to T70; if a tutorial line disagrees with this file, this file wins.

## Player actions
0. New Game opens a separate "New company" screen first: the player types a company name or presses Random, picks one of four CEO portraits (none is preselected; Start stays disabled until one is chosen), then Start begins the run (Back returns to the main menu). Menu text stays plain; no story terms.
1. Click a planet. The planet panel lists its resource rows (energy, food). Each row shows the Buy price, the Sell price and a vertical 5-line bar: how full the company storage is for that good (5 = full).
2. Buy and Sell on a row are standing local orders, each toggled on or off. No ship is needed. Each cycle a Buy order buys a set amount at the Buy price and adds it to the company storage on that planet; a Sell order sells stock from that storage at the Sell price. A full storage makes a Buy idle with the status "storage full" (not an error).
3. A route is optional and only moves stock between company storages. On a storage row the player presses "Send to..." and clicks the destination planet. This makes a one-way route with one ship, assigned automatically from the ships the company owns. If no ship is free, the screen says so. Routes are NOT made by clicking Buy here and Sell there.
4. Empty source: the route stalls and waits. Full destination with a Sell order or onward route there: the route waits for room (neutral). Full destination and nothing will ever make room: the route is blocked (red line, one notification) until the player acts.
5. Each planet panel lists its routes, each with a Remove button. Ship upkeep is shown plainly (revenue, goods cost, upkeep, trip cost, net) and the cash forecast lists Expenses.
6. Cycles end on a timer; the game starts paused after the preface; P pauses. The goal is the Star Bazaar: grow one world into a capital through Prosperity, build the Bazaar there stage by stage and open it with a final delivery before the term ends (market share was removed; houses send weather, not rivalry; there is no Competitors menu). The run ends with a market ending, or a loss or soft ending when the term ends first. Stranded (no ships, cash below a ship price) gives a one-time rescue payment, not a loss.
7. Skills are bought with expansion points (first planet visit, first route between a new pair, first route or storage in a new system). The Skills screen does not pause the game. Space Storage and Permit Charter are skills; the permit itself is bought in the system window (galaxy view, click a closed system), with its price and the reason it cannot be bought yet. The three Foundations come first: no market-tree skill can be learned until all three are learned. System 2 (Dunmere Reach) is open to buy with the Permit Charter (C1) and is in the Alpha; system 3 is planned but on hold, so keep story room for it (a line or two that points beyond system 2 is welcome, nothing the player can already act on).
8. Growth and the goal (T315). Each world has a need list. Sales that meet a need fill it; a world that is fed grows through tiers (Outpost, Village, Town, City, Metropolis), pays tax, and goes Restless if a need stays short. A world at the capital tier can host the Star Bazaar (one per run, final). Its stages need goods in the capital's storage and other worlds at a tier, later one in another system. The last stage posts the Opening, a gig: fly the charter cargo to the capital and dock to win. Market share no longer wins and there is no Competitors menu.
9. Guided tutorial order (T315): first flight (fly, build a post, wait), select a world, Buy, Send to, run a cycle, Ship upkeep, Sell, storage, read Needs, grow a world a tier, learn a Foundation, learn Permit Charter, then the goal (open the Star Bazaar panel). Later mechanics are one-time tips (TIP_* in story/screens.md), each shown the first time it matters.

## Events (planned, T12)
An event first appears as a small square icon with no text (hover shows the title); the game keeps running. A plain price change has a quiet icon that fades away if ignored. Any other event has a warm icon that stays until clicked. Clicking an icon pauses the game, moves the map to the planet the event names, highlights it, and opens a notification with the event text and an OK / Continue button. Some events offer two or three choices instead. Each choice has a short label and a plain effect line. The gameplay-programmer picks the effect from this vocabulary (no others): price up or down for one planet's commodity for a while; one planet closed for a while; a one-off cash gain or loss; a one-off change to storage stock of one good at one planet. Magnitudes are abstract and set later by the economy designer.
The first time the company has earned enough from trade for Space Storage, one more warm icon appears and stays until clicked. Its window says Space Storage is available and where to learn it, with one OK button. It comes once per run and changes nothing in the game.
