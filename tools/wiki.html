Logistics

Hubs

A hub is an automatic local logistics manager. It keeps nearby worlds supplied without requiring a manual route for every need.

How a hub works

Each cycle, a hub:

  1. Looks at established worlds within its reach and in the same solar system.
  2. Checks each unlocked need, such as Flowers.
  3. Subtracts deliveries already planned through your manual routes.
  4. Finds the cheapest eligible source within the hub's reach. Goods already in that planet's storage are preferred because they have no purchase price; otherwise, the hub buys at the planet's local price.
  5. Assigns one ship from the hub pool.
  6. Delivers up to the world's remaining demand, limited by that ship's capacity.
  7. Charges the purchase price, travel cost, and hub fee.

Rules and limits

  • A hub becomes available at prosperity tier 3.
  • Only one hub is allowed per solar system.
  • Current reach is 45 map units.
  • Current maximum pool is four ships.
  • Ships placed in the pool cannot simultaneously be used by manual routes.
  • A hub never uses Space Storage.
  • You can exclude an entire world or an individual need from automation.
  • Hub deliveries are temporary internal routes, so they do not appear in the normal route list.

Dispatching, not warehousing

The hub is not a warehouse or transfer station. Goods do not travel source → hub → destination. Instead, the hub dispatches a ship directly from the chosen source to the destination. The hub planet determines the center of its service area.

Current capacity limitation

One short need receives at most one hub ship per cycle. If Tillam needs 24 Flowers and a Dart carries 16, the hub delivers only 16—even when the pool has more idle ships. This is why a hub can show enough goods and ships while still leaving a need partially unfulfilled.

The expected improvement is to let multiple pooled ships serve the same need until it is filled or the pool is exhausted.
