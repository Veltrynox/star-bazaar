## 1. What changed from v1 and why

- **Share win, share graph, rival share: dropped** (owner answer 1). Share is still computed in Phase 1 so nothing breaks, but nothing reads it; it is removed in Phase 3.
- **Charter of Plenty (four tracks): replaced by the Star Bazaar.** Four bars are a checklist; one great building you raise at a capital of your choice is a goal the player wants, and it forces the same breadth (worlds, reach, industry) through one object with real trade-offs (section 4).
- **Sector spreading and sector guilds: cut.** The engine already limits a run to one branch, so the simpler replacement is the branch ladder: each world's needs climb your own branch's goods by goods-tier (owner answer 2).
- **Needs are met by sales, not by stock held** (v1 said "storage holds its needs"). Sales are what the engine counts; stock sitting in storage is not supply.
- **Need pricing is the saturation rule**: a need opens a buyer, hunger raises the price, sales above the need earn a surplus price. This replaces v1's vague "price pull" and the review's 7A/7B.
- **Reclaiming dead worlds: folded into Living Worlds** (owner answer 8). A dead world is a world at the "Barren" step with its own short need list; no separate mechanic, one more step on the same ladder.
- **Raw Sources (T253): shrunk.** Mining goods are Minerals-only after T239, so a merged raw-source system would serve one branch in four. It becomes one intensity lever for Minerals runs inside the Frontier section; dead worlds matter to every branch through raids and reclaim.
- **Houses: weather only** (owner answer 6). No standing, no contracts from houses, no opponent. v1's "house contract" gig kind is cut; shortages come from planets.
- **Hubs stay, re-aimed** (owner answer 7, on top of routes): a hub keeps worlds fed (growth upkeep), it does not chase profit. Manual routes stay the profit tool. This gives each layer its own job.
- **Systems as one unit with a stance (v1 Phase 4): cut.** A stance dial is a number tweak with no new verb. Systems matter through the Bazaar's reach requirement and the hub per system.
- **Ship type per hub / mixed fleets: parked.** Not needed for the loop; the Flagship carries the "ship feel".
- **Planet moods: kept, neutral** (owner answer 5). A face shows the lean per world; it never speeds or slows growth, so neither side of a chain choice becomes the dominant one.
- **Planets never lose a tier** (owner answer 4): neglect drains progress only.
- **Time pressure**: the term, the milestone win (Bazaar opened), a kind soft ending and free play are kept (owner answer 3); the term length is economy-designer's.
- **New: the Flagship** (owner idea A): the Pathfinder gains a hold, dock trade, a boost, and becomes the tool for gigs.
- **New: Living Traffic** (owner idea B): an instanced traffic layer that reads out the economy (lanes, tiers, hubs, weather, raids).
- **Phase 1 re-cut**: v1 shipped growth while share still decided the win (two masters, and growth would feel like a side system). v2 Phase 1 ships growth AND the Bazaar win together, so the first playable slice already answers "what am I playing for".
- **2x speed, survey-as-needs-reveal, permit-as-System-Charter: dropped** (already built, or no new decision).

## 2. The core loop and a great 30-minute session

### Core loop (five lines)
1. **Supply a world**: sell what it needs (by route, by hub, or by hand with the Flagship).
2. **It grows**: met needs fill its ring; a full ring is a tier-up: more lights, more traffic, more tax, a bigger and newer need list.
3. **Growth pulls you outward**: new needs name goods you do not make yet (workshops, skills) or sell better elsewhere (hungry prices move the best lane).
4. **Hand off the old work**: routes earn, hubs keep worlds fed, and your hands go to the Flagship's gigs and the next world.
5. **Raise the Star Bazaar** at your capital: each stage needs a bill of goods and a quorum of grown worlds, until the opening wins the run.

### A great 30 minutes (a Military run; 1 cycle is about 1 minute today; the session shows all phases built)
- **0:00-1:00** Paused start. Voltara (home, Outpost) is small and dim, two shuttles idle in orbit. The Next Up strip says "Fly the Flagship to Tillam". The player clicks the Flagship, clicks Tillam: the ship swings its nose round, the engines flare, a gold trail draws out. Unpause.
- **1:00-3:00** Arrival, a dock ping; the post is built: Tillam becomes an Outpost. Its card: "Tillam needs energy and powder", two empty notches on a ring around it in 3D, need icons hanging under it. The player sets Buy powder at Voltara, Send to Tillam, Sell at Tillam. A tiny freighter leaves Voltara with warm running lights (laden) and comes back dim (empty).
- **3:00-5:00** Cycle end: the energy notch fills; powder is short. The gig board pings: "Rush: Tillam wants powder within 2 cycles". The Flagship is docked at Voltara: the player opens the dock sheet, buys a hold of powder, flies, taps Boost on the straight leg (burns a little energy from the hold). Crates fly from the ship to the planet, the ring jumps two notches, a premium counts up.
- **5:00-8:00** Tillam reaches Settlement: a 0.4 s slow, a pulse ring, the night side lights up in a grid, three more shuttles spawn in orbit, a skill point pops. New needs: rivets (shown) and a "?" slot (a good not visible yet; Next Up: "A Military workshop would open Tillam's next need"). Tillam now buys rivets at a hungry price: Next Up shows the new best lane.
- **8:00-12:00** Exploration mode establishes Ashgate and Flintmoor while the player wires lanes. A red Harrow convoy drifts in and parks at Ashgate: the forecast chip says a powder flood is coming there. The player turns the Ashgate powder Sell off for two cycles and sends it to Tillam, whose powder need is already full: the card shows "surplus price" on the extra units, so the player splits instead. Prices are a live puzzle now.
- **12:00-16:00** Munitions Line built; rounds appear. Tillam reaches Town: "Found the Star Bazaar here?" Tillam is central and lush; Voltara is home and the crafted-goods buyer. The player picks Tillam. Scaffolding appears in Tillam's orbit. Stage 1 bill: powder, rivets, energy; quorum: two other worlds at Settlement.
- **16:00-20:00** A signal gig (GIG01: a weak beacon). The Flagship leaves its post work; on arrival, "Cut the hold free" or "Pull the flight log". Routes keep running behind it. Stage 1 completes: the first Bazaar ring segment locks into place, the sky's traffic briefly orbits the capital, and a wing choice (two neutral perks) appears.
- **20:00-24:00** The permit opens Dunmere. The Flagship jumps; Dunmere is dark and quiet: no lights, two shuttles. The contrast with home is the reward. Rush orders appear there; the player carries the first supply by hand to kick-start an Outpost.
- **24:00-28:00** Voltara reaches Town and becomes a hub. Its reach ring ripples out; shuttles fan out to Ashgate and Flintmoor, and the player deletes three small manual lanes. Next Up: "Bazaar stage 2: rounds short, and a Settlement in Dunmere".
- **28:00-30:00** Raiders slip out of Cinderrest and hit the Voltara-Tillam lane; two cargo pods drift and start to fade. The player boosts the Flagship to scoop them. Then looks at Cinderrest's card: its reclaim bill would end the raids and give a new world, but the goods are needed for stage 2. That decision is the cliffhanger. Three worlds glow, Bazaar stage 1 of 4, the sky is busy, and the player does not want to stop.

## 3. The mechanics

Five mechanics survive, plus one presentation system that carries information. Every one names its verbs, decisions, feedback, scaling, failure, links and juice.

### 3.1 Living Worlds (Prosperity) — the spine

**What it is.** Every established world has a Prosperity tier. Tier 0 is discovered (no post). Building the post founds the Outpost (tier 1). Higher tiers (names by story-designer; count by economy-designer) come from meeting needs. Dead worlds sit one step below, at Barren (section 3.4).

**Needs, by rule (data/prosperity.json, small):**
- A world of tier T needs: energy, plus the run's branch goods whose goods-tier is at most T, minus the goods the world itself sells (its exports never become its needs), up to a per-tier count. The planet type orders the list and weights demand (an agrarian world wants energy first, a volcanic one food-like goods of the branch). A per-planet override exists for hand tuning but is not required.
- Each need has a **demand**: units per cycle, scaled by tier (economy-designer keys).
- A need whose good is not visible yet (Availability) shows as a locked "?" slot. It counts as unmet. The Next Up strip names the skill or workshop that would reveal something, never the good (disclosure rule).

**Fill.** Units of the good sold AT that world in the cycle (Sell orders, hub deliveries, Flagship hand sales). Hooked in `route_engine._run_local()` where sales are already noted per planet.

**Growth.** At cycle end, if every need is met, the world's progress gains a step; if any is short, progress loses a smaller step (decay; never below 0). Full progress is a tier-up. A world never loses a tier. Steps per tier and the decay are economy keys.

**Need pricing (the saturation rule).**
- A need **opens a buyer**: if the world had no sell price for that good, it now has one (the need price for that tier).
- **Hunger**: each cycle a need is short, its sell price rises a step, up to a cap; met, it settles back.
- **Surplus**: units sold above the demand in a cycle earn a surplus price (a share of the normal one).
- Effect: the best lane moves as worlds grow and fill; dumping one good on one world stops paying; selling where it is needed pays. This is the fix for "the best lane is best forever" (review 7A/7B), told in a cause the player understands (a hungry world).
- Owner question 3 asks whether surplus applies to need goods only (recommended) or every sale.

**Rewards per tier.** A tax income each cycle (growth pays, apart from margins); skill points at the first time any world reaches a tier (replacing share-milestone points; never bought with credits); bigger demand (bigger sales); the next goods-tier on the need list; at a set tier, the world may become a hub or the Bazaar capital; the world counts as a province for the Bazaar quorum.

**Moods (the lean, visible).** Each world wears a face (warm, plain, cold; art by ui-programmer, words by story-designer). It is set by the chain choices whose events named that world and by dark sales there (EV52-55). It picks GOOD or BAD at the end and flavours which signal gigs appear. It **never changes growth, prices or tax**: a mood that sped growth would make one side of every chain choice the right answer.

**Player verbs.** Read the card; set Sell orders and lanes into needs; choose which world to push; time a surplus split; carry a rush by hand; pick a capital.

**Decisions and trade-offs.**
- Concentrate or spread: all needs must be met to progress, so feeding three worlds half-way grows none; feeding one fully grows it but leaves others decaying.
- Profit or growth: the hungry price is tempting at a world that is not your growth target; the steady need at your target pays less but builds tiers and tax.
- Surplus: one fat lane into one world now loses value above demand; the player splits lanes or finds a second buyer.
- Locked slot: spend skill points and cash on the workshop that opens the next tier, or grow other worlds first.

**Feedback.** A ring of notches around each world in 3D (progress), need icons under it (met bright, short pulsing amber, locked "?"); card rows (need, demand, sold this cycle, hunger arrow or "surplus" chip); night-side city lights and halo per tier (fx); orbital shuttles per tier (traffic); a Restless amber ring when progress decays.

**Scaling.** First minutes: one world, two needs. Mid: four to six worlds, three needs each, crafted goods enter at tier 3. Late: ten-plus worlds with four or five needs each, too many to wire by hand, which is the cue for hubs.

**Failure states.** Restless (progress draining) is the only failure; it is visible, gentle, and recoverable. Locked needs stall a world until the player opens the good: the strip says what kind of step would help.

**Connections.** Feeds the Bazaar (quorum, capital), the Flagship (rush orders come from worlds close to a tier-up or decaying), hubs (keep needs met), traffic (density shows tier), weather (shortages raise demand), the Frontier (reclaim is the Barren step).

**Juice moments.** Need met: a notch ticks with a small chime. Tier-up: 0.4 s time-slow, pulse ring, camera eases toward the world, the night side blooms, shuttles launch, a rising chime per tier, the skill-point pop. Restless: the ring dims and a shuttle or two leave.

**Why it is worth it:** one rule set gives the game its goal, its sinks, its saturation, its moving best lane and its readable progress.
**Biggest risk:** balance runaway (tax plus hungry prices) or stalls (locked slots too early). Mitigation: economy keys per tier, a hunger cap, and a placeholder ladder that the owner tunes by hand.

### 3.2 The Flagship and Gigs — micro play with the 3D ship

**What it is.** The Pathfinder becomes the Flagship (name by story-designer): still the scout that discovers and establishes, now with a **small hold**, **dock trade**, a **boost**, and the **gig board** as its job list. The company still runs on routes; the Flagship is the captain's own ship for the jobs that routes cannot do well: urgent, one-off, far or strange.

**Pillar change (owner question 1).** "The player never buys or sells cargo by hand" becomes "The company runs on routes; the captain flies for special jobs". Guard rails: the hold is small; routine hand trade must earn less per cycle than one lane of the same good (economy rule); the premium lives in gigs.

**Verbs.**
- **Fly**: select the Flagship, click a world, station, asteroid pack, gig marker or open space (T200 controls). Right-click menu: Establish, Dock and trade, Take gig here.
- **Boost**: hold a key or the boost button: top speed and acceleration up, burning energy from the hold. Boost charges refill slowly (keys). Turning stays limited, so boost pays on straight legs: a small skill element of line choice.
- **Dock sheet** at an established world (pauses the clock, like an event window): hold rows; Buy and Sell at the world's prices (need pricing applies: a hungry world pays more, surplus pays less); **Unload to storage** and **Load from storage** (free moves between the hold and company storage: seed a world's Sell order, collect a stranded stock).
- **Gig board**: a short list of open offers (max count is a key). Accept one: a marker appears and the Flagship can be sent at once.
- **Explore**: the existing exploration mode stays as the Flagship's "idle job".
- **Jump**: to an open system.

**Gig kinds (one engine, data/gigs.json, conditions via game/conditions.gd):**
| Kind | Comes from | The player does | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signal (story) | The T216 rules and story texts (GIG01, GIG02, more later) | Fly to the marker; on arrival pick one of two neutral options | Goods lot, one-off perk, knowledge or a story line (T216 rules; never skill points) |
| Rush order | A world near its tier-up, Restless, or hit by a shortage (weather) | Deliver a lot of a need good before a deadline | A premium price, and the delivery counts as that need met for some cycles (progress the routes could not give in time) |
| Salvage (Phase 5) | Cargo pods dropped by a raid | Reach the pods before they fade; they load into the hold | The lost goods back |
| Opening (Phase 5) | The last Bazaar stage | Fly the charter cargo to the capital | The win |

**Decisions and trade-offs.**
- Which offer: premium against distance, deadline against flight time (ETA is shown on the board), and against the Flagship's other work (establishing a post, exploring a new system). The Flagship is one ship: its time is the price.
- Hold split: cargo or fuel. More energy carried means more boost and less cargo.
- Deliver or sell: a rush counts for growth; selling the same lot at the hungriest world earns more cash now.
- Multi-stop runs: buy at A for B's rush, load B's stranded stock for C. Planning a loop is the "judgment" reward.
- Seed or sell: unload to storage to let a Sell order spread the units over cycles (no surplus), or sell all at once.

**How it rewards skill and judgment.** Reading the board (good picks earn more than taking all), routing under deadlines, boost timing on straight legs, and multi-stop chaining. There is no combat and no twitch test; the skill is planning in real time on the game clock, with pause always allowed.

**Avoiding busywork as the empire grows.**
- Gigs are always optional. The win never needs a hand trip except the one Opening flight.
- The board holds few offers; new ones replace the weakest; expiry is free (T216).
- Rush orders come from the worlds that matter (closest to a tier-up, Restless, shortage), not from every world, and a world that is fed by a hub posts fewer.
- Late gigs grow in stakes, not in number: system-2 and later worlds have bigger lots and deadlines that need a jump.
- Routine hand trade earns less than a lane: once routes and hubs work, the Flagship goes back to being special.

**Feedback.** The selection ring, the destination line (T248), a gig marker ring that pulses slower as the deadline nears, the deadline bar on the board, the ETA from `eta_cycles()`, hold readout on the ship panel, a boost gauge, a "delivered" stamp on the world's need row.

**Scaling.** Early: the Flagship establishes posts and does one rush per few cycles. Mid: signals, rushes in two systems. Late: the hub runs the upkeep, the Flagship does big rushes, salvage dashes, and the Opening.

**Failure states.** A missed deadline: the offer is gone, nothing else is lost (the world just did not get its extra progress). No cash: dock Buy is refused with the usual notice. A full hold: Buy limited, the sheet says so. The Flagship is never destroyed and raids never target it.

**Connections.** Living Worlds (rush fills progress; hand sales count as need fill), weather (shortages spawn rushes), Frontier (salvage, raids), Bazaar (Opening), traffic (the Flagship is the one hero ship among instanced traffic).

**Juice moments.** Order: the selection ring pops, the nose swings, an engine flare. Boost: a bright burst and a stretched trail (T246 two-engine trails). Dock: the ship eases into a docking ring, a clamp click. Trade: crates stream as small sprites from the ship to the world, a credit count-up. Rush saved: the need notch flashes and a short sting. Deadline near: the marker ring pulses faster and goes amber.

**Why it is worth it:** it gives the player hands-on moments between investment decisions, makes gigs (already texted) real, and gives the empire's growth a face (you personally saved Tillam's tier-up).
**Biggest risk:** hand trade outearning routes, which turns the company game into a courier game. Mitigation: the economy rule above and a small hold; owner checks it by hand.

### 3.3 Hubs — growth on autopilot (on top of routes)

**What it is.** A world at the hub tier can be made a **hub**. A hub keeps the needs of worlds in its **reach** supplied, buying from the cheapest established source in its reach and delivering with ships from its own **pool**. It feeds needs up to their demand only: it never sells surplus and never chases profit. Profit stays with the player's manual routes; hubs keep the empire alive while the player's attention moves on.

**Engine.** Hub deliveries are ordinary route records with an owner tag (`owner: hub:<planet>`) that the hub creates, resizes and retires each cycle; they settle with the normal route engine. Manual routes into a world count first; the hub fills only the gap.

**Verbs.** Designate a hub (one per system to start; more from a Bazaar wing or a skill); assign ships from the free fleet to its pool; untick a world or a need to keep it manual; release a hub.

**Decisions and trade-offs.**
- Placement: reach is limited (by hub tier), so a central world serves more but may be a poor source itself; the capital as a hub saves Bazaar haulage but uses its slot.
- Ships: every pool ship is one fewer for profit lanes.
- Cost: hub deliveries pay buy price, trip cost and a hub fee: less efficient than a tuned manual lane. Automation is bought with margin, so the player still hand-builds the lanes that matter.
- Scope: leave a world off the hub to grow it by hand (rush orders and tuned lanes) or let the hub hold it steady.

**Feedback.** A reach ring on the map; hub shuttles flying hub to worlds (traffic, section 3.6); per-need status on each card: "fed by your route", "fed by hub", "short: no source in reach / no free ship in pool / no cash"; a hub panel listing served worlds and gaps.

**Scaling.** None in the first 15 minutes. The first hub around the first Town. Late: one per system; a hub can draw from a Space Storage in its system (this answers T190's "storage as route source" for hubs only).

**Failure states.** Pool too small or cash short: some needs go short and the world turns Restless, with the reason on the card. The hub never runs debt (it stops buying when cash is under the ship cash floor already used by routes).

**Connections.** Keeps Living Worlds growing; frees the Flagship; carries Bazaar bills when the capital is in reach; its lanes can be raided (Frontier).

**Juice moments.** Designate: a ripple runs out to the reach edge and shuttles launch in a fan. A gap filled: a shuttle peels off toward the world. Release: shuttles return and dock.

**Why it is worth it:** without it the needs ladder becomes late-game busywork (ten worlds x four needs of wiring); with it, the player's attention moves up, as the owner asked (trade, route, hub).
**Biggest risk:** "the game plays itself". Mitigation: the hub fee, the pool cost and the no-profit rule keep the best money in the player's hands.

### 3.4 The Frontier — dead worlds as raid nests to reclaim (with Minerals mining)

**What it is.** Dead worlds (Cinderrest, Greywake, any later one) are Barren: the step below Outpost on the same Prosperity ladder. Left alone they are **raid nests**; reclaimed they become **living worlds**; for a Minerals run they are also **mines**.

**Raids (T250 re-scoped).** While a dead world in a system is not reclaimed, raiders (the T250 pack, pirate model, no combat) sortie at times against your lanes in that system. Chance rises with your traffic in the system and with mining intensity there (keys). A raid steals part of a trip, puts a malus on that lane for some cycles, and drops **cargo pods** that drift and fade: a Salvage gig for the Flagship. The Null Core's immunity now protects against something real.

**Reclaim (the Barren step).** A dead world's card shows a reclamation need list (energy and the run's branch goods by the same rule, plus nullglass or plate where the branch can trade them). Meeting it fills the same ring; when full, the world turns living (a new type with its own look), becomes an Outpost at tier 1, and its raids stop for good. While being reclaimed, it cannot be mined.

**Mining intensity (Minerals runs only; the shrunk T253).** Each dead world and asteroid cluster shows remaining stock and cycles left; a three-step intensity lever (gentle, normal, hard) trades yield against danger and raid chance. Asteroid clusters keep the "never refill" rule (the unused refill and survey keys go in T255).

**Decisions.** Reclaim now (a goods bill competing with the Bazaar, the end of raids, a new province) or later. For Minerals: mine (the raws that crafted goods need, with raids) or reclaim (safety and a world, no more dross or nullglass there). For everyone: where to run lanes (a system with a nest is riskier) and whether to chase pods.

**Feedback.** The nest shows a faint red haze and pirate ships parked at it; a raid warning chip one cycle ahead with the Forecast skills; lane malus marker with cycles left; pods as blinking sprites with a fade ring.

**Scaling.** Nests matter from the first lanes in a system with a dead world; reclaim fits mid to late game, when tier-2 goods flow; Minerals players meet the lever from Prospecting on.

**Failure states.** Raids cost a share of a trip and a few cycles of a malus; no planet loses tier, no ship is destroyed.

**Connections.** Living Worlds (one ladder), Flagship (salvage), Bazaar (a reclaimed world is a province), traffic (raiders are drawn), skills (Null Core, Cold Survey, Deep Cut, Prospecting keep their roles).

**Juice moments.** Raid: raiders dart out, a flash on the hit ship, pods tumble out. Reclaim: a green wave crosses the surface, clouds form, the first lights come on, the nest's red haze blows away.

**Why it is worth it:** dead worlds become a decision for all four branches (today they matter to Minerals only), and reclaim is the strongest "the empire makes life" image, without a new system.
**Biggest risk:** raids feel unfair. Mitigation: warning chip, capped malus, pods give part back, and reclaim ends them for good.

### 3.5 Market weather (folded into events)

Not a separate mechanic: the existing event pipeline, re-voiced and made visible. Three named houses (Harrow, Velvet, Ostrel; temperaments by story-designer) are weather only (owner answer 6): no standing, no contracts, no opponent.
- **Flood** (one house): a world's sell price for a good dips for some cycles.
- **Shortage** (another): a world's demand for one of its needs jumps for some cycles, and its hunger rises (often spawns a Rush order from that world).
- **Congestion** (the third): trip cost rises on lanes at one world or a system exit for some cycles.
- **Telegraph:** a tinted house convoy flies to the world one cycle before (longer with Forecast skills). The convoy is the forecast: readable without opening a panel.
- Decisions: ride it (sell into the shortage, rush it) or dodge it (redirect away from the flood or the congested exit).
- Rival-hitting skill effects (`bot_strength_pct`, `bot_growth_mult`) become weather effects (softer weather for the player, longer forecast). Chain options that "hit a rival" become "delay or soften a house's next weather". Bots' volume curves, the Competitors panel and the share graph are removed in Phase 3.

### 3.6 Living Traffic — a sky that reports the economy

**Rule:** traffic is a readout of the books, never a simulation that changes them. Every drawn ship either is a real thing (Flagship, raiders, pods) or stands for a real state (a lane, a tier, a hub, a weather). Ships are drawn only in the viewed system; the economy runs everywhere as now.

**What is drawn and what it says.**
| Layer | Drawn as | It tells the player |
|---|---|---|
| Your lane ships | Small instanced models along the lane's arc, one per ship on the lane (today's dots) | Lane size (count), laden out (warm running lights) vs empty back (dim), stalled (parked at source, amber blink), blocked (red hazard light), the good (tint of the cargo pod) |
| Hub shuttles | Smaller instanced craft hub to served worlds | Which worlds a hub feeds, and which it cannot (no shuttle, amber icon) |
| Orbital traffic | Tiny shuttles circling and landing at each world | The world's tier (density) and health (thinning when Restless) |
| House convoys | Tinted small groups (Harrow red, Velvet violet, Ostrel green as today) | Weather coming to that world next cycle |
| Raiders and pods | The pirate model (T250), pods as blinking sprites | Danger now, salvage chance |
| Through traffic | A thin stream at system exits | Open trade links between systems (a Storage Link shows a heavier stream) |
| The Flagship | The full GLB with two-engine trails (T246) | The player's own ship |

**Performance budget on a Mac (targets for the programmer to measure, not promises).**
- Reference machine: the owner's Apple Silicon Mac, windowed 1080p; whole-frame target 16.6 ms (60 fps) with the system view at late game.
- Instanced traffic in the viewed system: design for 300 visible instances, stay smooth at a stress level of 1,000.
- Rough split: lane ships up to ship cap plus type bonus (about 12 today), hub shuttles up to 40, orbital traffic up to 30 per world (about 180 for six worlds), house convoys up to 30, through traffic up to 50, raiders up to 10.
- Draw calls: one MultiMesh per traffic mesh per layer, aiming at 12 or fewer draw calls for all traffic.
- Meshes: low-poly proxies of the corvette and pirate models for traffic, aiming at 300 triangles or fewer each (MultiMesh has no per-instance LOD).
- CPU: zero per-ship nodes. Lane, hub and orbital ships move in the vertex shader (Bezier or orbit from per-instance custom data, as the dot shader does today); instance buffers are written only when a lane or tier changes. Free-flying ones (convoys, raiders) use `Motion.step` on the CPU, few in number. CPU cost of traffic aiming at under 0.5 ms per frame.
- Trails: instanced ships use a short additive streak in the same shader (no `ImmediateMesh`); ribbon trails only for the Flagship and raiders.
- A debug-menu readout (instances per layer, draw calls) so the owner can measure.

**Why it is worth it:** the player sees the empire they built, and can read lane health, tiers, hubs and weather at a glance, which replaces several panels.
**Biggest risk:** clutter that hides the planets and lines. Mitigation: small scale (T252 ship scale key applies), far LOD to dots, and orbital traffic kept inside a ring close to the world.

## 4. The win condition and endings (final recommendation)

### The hard look at the Charter of Plenty
Four tracks (Worlds, Reach, Industry, Renewal) are four progress bars. Each bar is filled by doing what the game already asks, so they add no decision; the player ticks them off. Nothing is chosen, nothing is risked, and none of them is something to look at. **Verdict: replace it.**

### The Star Bazaar (working name; story-designer names it)
The game is called Star Bazaar: the win is building it.
- **Found it.** When a world first reaches the capital tier (a key; Town is the placeholder), the player may found the Bazaar there. One capital per run, final (owner question 5). The capital's type shapes its later needs; its place shapes haulage.
- **Raise it in stages** (count by economy-designer; four is the placeholder). Each stage has:
  - a **bill** of goods from the run's branch ladder, climbing goods-tiers by stage (the last stage needs the top crafted good), delivered into the capital's storage by route, hub or Flagship and **consumed** when the stage completes;
  - a **quorum**: a number of other worlds at a tier (breadth);
  - from a set stage, **reach**: at least one quorum world in another system.
- **Wings.** Each completed stage offers a choice between two wings (neutral perks; numbers by economy-designer, text by story-designer), for example a bigger Flagship hold against a longer hub reach, or more tax against cheaper workshops. The run's shape comes from these picks.
- **Opening.** When the last stage completes, the Opening gig appears: the Flagship flies the charter cargo to the capital. On docking, the traffic of the system converges on the capital, the Bazaar lights up, the run is won.

**Why players want it.** It is one visible thing that grows in orbit with every stage, it carries the game's name, and it is theirs (their capital, their wings).

**Hard choices it creates.**
- Capital choice: central world or rich one, early or wait for a better candidate.
- Every bill is a sink: the same goods that grow provinces and earn cash. Build a stage now (cash and growth slow down) or grow first (more income later, less term left).
- Quorum against depth: the bill wants a strong capital, the quorum wants many grown worlds.
- Reach: a second system is required, with its own nests and weather.
- Reclaim a dead world (a province, the end of raids) or put those goods into the Bazaar.

### Term, soft ending, free play
- The run keeps a term (the existing last-cycle key; economy-designer may lengthen it for a growth game).
- **Win:** the Opening, any time before the term ends.
- **Soft ending:** the term ends first: "The Unfinished Bazaar" (story-designer), its epilogue scaled by stages done. Kind, never a loss.
- **Free play:** after the win or the soft ending, the player may continue with no further ending; the Bazaar panel keeps counting.
- **Loss:** Bankrupt stays. Swept is removed (no share). Outcompeted becomes the soft ending above.

### Endings
- The branch picks the ending family (one branch per run after T239: MIL, LUX, MIN, BIO), the lean picks GOOD or BAD, as today: the 8 win endings stay.
- The lean is readable through the worlds' moods, and an end recap names the three choices that decided it.
- Epilogue lines speak of the capital, the worlds grown, the dead world reclaimed (if any), and the wings chosen.

2. **Win.** Replace the Charter of Plenty (four tracks) with the Star Bazaar: a capital you found, stages with goods bills, a quorum of grown worlds, reach to a second system, wing choices, and an Opening flight. **Recommend: yes.** It asks for the same breadth as the four tracks, but as one thing the player wants to build, and each stage is a choice between spending goods now and growing first.
3. **Saturation.** Should units sold above a world's need earn a surplus price? **Recommend: yes, for need goods only** at first; the key `need_surplus_all` lets you try it on every sale later without code.
4. **Phase 1 cut.** Ship growth and the Bazaar win together, with share hidden but still computed until Phase 3, instead of v1's "growth while share still decides"? **Recommend: yes.** If the run had two goals, growth would feel like a side system and the first playtest would judge the wrong game.
5. **Capital.** Is the Bazaar capital chosen once and final, from the first world that reaches the capital tier or later? **Recommend: once and final.** Being able to move it would remove the hardest early choice.
6. **Term and moods.** Keep a term (economy-designer may lengthen it for a growth game) with free play after, and keep moods neutral (a face only, no effect on growth)? **Recommend: yes to both.** If moods sped up growth, one side of every chain choice would become the right answer.
