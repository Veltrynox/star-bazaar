# Gigs: random jobs for the Pathfinder, each with a story

Date: 2026-11-06. Task T216. Author: feature-designer. Status: waits for the owner. No numbers here (economy-designer pins them in data/); no story text (story-designer). One proposal, ranked 1 of 1.

## Problem
After the first posts are built, the 3D Pathfinder has little to do. Trade runs on its own, and the scout sits idle or in exploration mode. Flying has no story and no surprise.

## Idea
A gig is a one-time signal that shows up in the Pathfinder's current system: a wrecked freighter, a drifting beacon, a stranded prospector, a customs drone. The player flies there, a short window opens with a story and two neutral choices, and each choice gives something and costs something. Each gig is unique, appears at most once per run, and is tied to the player's branch and progress through conditions. No gig gives skill points. Gigs never touch cash for skill, and no gig is needed to win.

## The player's new choice
Whether to pull the scout off a build or exploration to chase a signal that might expire, and which of two offers to take on arrival. The scout's time is the price: while it flies a gig it builds and surveys nothing.

## How a gig appears
- Eligible = its `when` tree holds and it has not been done or skipped. Checked once per cycle. Pick one eligible gig by weight (data) with a seeded draw, so a run's list is stable per save.
- At most one gig is open at a time. A minimum gap between gigs (balance), and a per-run cap (balance). The first gig never before the first post stands (cycle floor from balance).
- Marker: a pulsing ring on the 3D map at a spot in the Pathfinder's current system, plus one small gig icon in the event icon strip (no pause, no popup; it never blocks events). Clicking the icon moves the map to the marker. The icon text says only "Signal" and the system; the story stays hidden until arrival.
- Expiry: the signal fades after a number of cycles (balance), on the game clock, so pause stops it. Expiry is free: no penalty, no log of a missed gig. A gig that expires is marked skipped; it does not come back this run (story gigs are rare and each gets one chance).
- Moving to another system closes an open gig of the old system (same free expiry).

## What the player does
1. Order the Pathfinder to the marker (same order path as a planet order; flight time is the same cost model).
2. On arrival the window opens and the game pauses, like an event window. The Pathfinder holds at the site while the window is open.
3. Pick one of two options (a gig with no choice is allowed for pure story; at most a few of these). Effects apply, a short line shows what was taken and given, the Pathfinder is free again.

## What it costs
- Flight time of the scout (it is off its job, so a build under way is cancelled by the existing rule: credits not refunded; the order is refused while a build runs, with a notice, so the player is never trapped by accident).
- Option costs (examples: a repair stop with the scout idle for some cycles, a small cash fee, a lot of goods handed over, a rival noticing).
- Never the fleet: ships keep running; gigs do not stop, slow or risk a trade lane.

## What it gives (unique per branch, never skill points)
Each reward is one of four kinds. Value of every gig sits in one shared band per stage (economy-designer), so no branch gets a richer pool.
| Kind | Example | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Goods lot | A salvaged crate of a good of your branch | Only a good that is visible now (Availability). Lands in a planet or space storage at once (open question 2). |
| Perk, one-off | A free scout survey, a cheaper next post, a look at next event, a lane's first trip free | Small, one use or a few cycles; cash and time only, no share effect (like CEO stats). |
| Knowledge | A price view of a planet not yet open; a hint of a sell price | Information only; no rule changes. |
| Story line | A new line in the log and a short fragment that fills a branch's world | Text only; nothing to farm. |

Forbidden rewards: skill points, a share jump, a win-chain step, an ending flag, a permanent multiplier. A gig can read the hidden chain lean to pick WHICH story it offers but must never show or move the lean (see below).

## Gigs per branch (examples; the story-designer writes the list, about six per branch plus a few shared)
Each gig is tied to a branch (`branch_is`, from the branch-goods proposal or the CEO's stored branch today), and a stage (cycle, systems opened, skills, chain steps).
| Branch | Gig (working title) | Appears when | Options (give / cost) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Military | Wrecked escort | system 1 open, a post stands, early | Strip the guns: a goods lot of the branch starter, the next lane trips cost a little more. Report it: cash fee in, a rival notes you. |
| Military | Dead drop in the shipyard belt | Munitions Line built | Take the crate: a lot of a Munitions good, scout idle for a while. Return it: knowledge of a buyer price. |
| Luxury | Perfume freighter adrift | Luxury visible, system 1 | Claim the cargo: a lot of blooms or silk. Tow it to a port: a cheaper next post. |
| Luxury | A noble's lost yacht | system 2 open | Rescue: a story line and a one-use sale bonus at one planet. Ignore: nothing. |
| Minerals | Prospector's claim | dead world with a platform | Buy the map: knowledge of the next cluster. Haul his ore: a lot of ore, scout away longer. |
| Minerals | Null-glass echo | Greywake discovered | Listen: a story line and a price view. Cut a sample: a nullglass lot, a repair stop. |
| Biotech | Quarantined drifter | food or spores traded | Open the hold: a lot of spores, risk handled as a cash fee. Seal and report: knowledge and a small perk. |
| Biotech | Seed vault beacon | seeds visible | Recover seed stock: a lot; or restore the beacon: a story line. |
| Shared | Old customs drone | a skill from a Foundation tree learned | Pay the toll: knowledge. Outrun it: scout idle. |
| Shared | Chain-aware pair | a chain step fired (either pick) | Two gigs, one per pick, so each path gets its own after-story with no word of right or wrong. |
Skills tie in through `skill_learned`: a gig may open only for a player who learned a node, and offer a flavour of it, never a number beyond that node's own scope.

## How it stays neutral (no dominant strategy)
- One open gig, a minimum gap and a per-run cap stop gig farming.
- Every gig is one-time; no repeat, no re-roll, no grinding a signal.
- Every option has a gain and a cost. Time of the scout is a real cost, so a run that sends the scout to every gig builds and surveys later; a run that ignores gigs is never behind on the win.
- Rewards sit in one value band per stage and carry no share effect, no skill points and no permanent power. A goods lot is small against a normal lane, so it can not replace trade.
- Gigs the player cannot use never appear: every good and feature named in a gig must pass its own `good_visible` or feature rule.
- Hidden lean: a gig reads chain choices only as an eligibility condition and never changes the lean; its text never says good or bad. Cash-for-skill stays impossible: no gig option sells or grants a skill point.
- Dark-sale spotting (branch events): a gig option never reuses that rule.

## Discovery, exploration mode and pause
- Gigs appear only in the system the Pathfinder is in, and only with discovery on (`discovery_enabled` 1). At 0 the scout is inert, so no gigs.
- A gig site is not a planet: it never counts as discovered or established and gives no first-visit skill point.
- Exploration mode never flies to a gig by itself. A manual gig order ends the mode, like any manual order. A gig does not block resuming it.
- Pause: the signal timer and the flight follow the game clock. The gig window pauses on arrival; closing it resumes. An open event window has priority: a gig arrival while an important event waits shows after the event is answered.
- Save: open gig, its remaining time, done and skipped ids and the seed live in the save under "gigs".

## Data shape (data/gigs.json) and conditions
```
{ "gigs": [ { "id": "MIL_G1", "weight": 1, "stage": 1,
  "when": { "all": [ "branch_is:military", "post_built:planet_2", {"not": "gig_done:MIL_G1"} ] },
  "system": 1,
  "expires": "balance.gig_expire_cycles",
  "title": "gig_mil_g1_title", "body": "gig_mil_g1_body",
  "options": [ { "id": "a", "text": "gig_mil_g1_a", "visible_when": "...",
                 "gives": [ {"kind": "goods", "good": "powder", "lot": "small"} ],
                 "costs": [ {"kind": "scout_idle", "size": "short"} ] } ] } ] }
```
- All text values are ids in data/story_text.json, written by story-designer from `story/*.md` and built by `python3 tools/build_story.py` (the generated file is never edited by hand). Gigs keep mechanics and ids only.
- Lot sizes, band and cost sizes are named tiers ("small", "short"), pinned by economy-designer in data/balance.json.
- Conditions use game/conditions.gd. Today's atoms cover: `skill_learned`, `workshop_built`, `system_open`, `cycle_at_least`, `stock_held`, `good_visible`, `good_traded`, `event_fired`, `planet_visited`, `post_built`, `platform_built`.
- New atoms needed (gameplay-programmer): `branch_is:<id>`, `gig_done:<id>`, `chain_pick:<step>:<option>` (for chain direction, read from Chains.picks), `market_share_at_least:<balance>` (own share; for stage gating, since today's share atoms need a chain step), `pathfinder_in_system:<n>`.
- Availability: any goods reward goes through one rule: a gig is eligible only if every good in its `gives` and its title or body ids passes `good_visible`. A data lint in `_test_availability` adds: no gig `when` may be true at the start state, and a text scan of eligible gigs at start finds no hidden good name.

## Risk
- The scout is a thin slot of the game; too many gigs cheapen discovery and make the build rhythm noisy. Keep the gap and cap generous.
- Goods lots could slip past the branch-goods rule (a Military lot of luxury goods). Lint: a goods reward must belong to the branch in `when`, or be energy.
- Chain-aware gigs can leak the hidden lean if text hints at it. Story-designer review needed.
- Rewards that count toward share could feed the win. Recommend they count as normal sales only after they are sold (question 3).
